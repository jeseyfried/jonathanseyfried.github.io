## Today’s Document 
- Subject: instructions for meeting Isabella de Mallorca at the border crossing
- Date: March 16, 1387 (the March 17th letter on this folio is just pleasantries to the King of Castile)
- Day of the Week: Sunday
- Language: Catalan
- Archival Reference Number: ACA CR R1751 f26v
- [Link to PARES](https://pares.mcu.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/show/4801766)
- Place: Barcelona
- Sender: Joan
- Recipient: Governor of Roussillon

## Historical Thinking Notes
- Sourcing: Joan chose a very high ranking person, the governor, to handle this task, not merely a bailiff; as king, Joan concerned himself with the movements of any political rival, even if their power had diminished to very little, such as the case with Isabella de Mallorca

- Contextualization: Joan's father, Pere the Ceremonious, waged a bitter war against Isabella de Majorca's father in order to keep the island a territory within the Crown of Aragon; a later attempt at a breakaway rebellion resulted in the death of Isabell's brother, James IV of Majorca, in 1375; by the time of this journey, Isabella de Mallorca probably lacked any substantial political leverage

- Corroboration: the document examined in [Episode 61](e61-feb23) corroborates this one in Joan's attention to the details of Isabella de Majorca's visit to the Crown of Aragon

- Close-Reading: the probata statements on this page merited close attention, as the scribe is Bartolomeo Sirvent, one of Violant's principle scribes; possibly Sirvent's presence in Joan's register is an indication that Joan and Violant worked on a document together

## What is this document doing?
- This document establishes close monitoring of a traveler, starting at a border crossing point.
- The document moves the apparatus of the state to track a person's movements.

## Questions
- Did Joan want to be informed of Isabella's movements because he did not trust her? Or was it for her protection? Both?
- Was it standard practice for all high status visitors to pass through a single check point, such as this fort? 
- Was the Salses Fort a required checkpoint for everyone entering the Crown of Aragon from southern France?
- How did Isabella de Mallorca feel about her upcoming journey into the Crown of Aragon? Apprehensive?

## Additional Notes
The Salses Fort still exists today but the [Wikipedia page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_de_Salses) says that it is a fifteenth-century construction. The remnant of older structures within fifteenth century constructions also came up for the mill discussed in [Episode 44](e44-feb6).
