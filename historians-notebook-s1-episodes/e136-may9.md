## Today’s Document 
- Subject: expression of displeasure at the poor job performance of a knight
- Date: May 9, 1387
- Day of the Week: Thursday
- Language: Catalan
- Archival Reference Number: ACA CR R1819 f98v-99r
- [Link to PARES](https://pares.mcu.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/show/4801968)
- Place: Barcelona
- Sender: Violant
- Recipient: Berenguer d'Olms

## Historical Thinking Notes
- Sourcing: apparently the knight Berenguer d'Olms considered Violant's commission optional; Violant, for her part, had a financial interest in ensuring that the tax collection being carried out by Jacme Quinta proceeded successfully

- Contextualization: when conceptualizing women's power in the Middle Ages, many complications arise and a very helpful article by Marie Kelleher helps to navigate through thorny topics like 'soft versus hard power' and the intersection of social class with patriarchy in medieval societies[^mk]; queen lieutenants possessed a great deal of authority both legally and de facto in the late medieval Crown of Aragon

[^mk]:Marie A. Kelleher, “What Do We Mean by 'Women and Power'?” *Medieval Feminist Forum: A Journal of Gender and Sexuality* 51, no. 2 (2016): 104–15.

- Corroboration: the document examined in [Episode 64](e64-feb26) offers an interesting corroboration as that February 26th document is the original commission Violant sent to Berenguer d'Olms to commission him to join Jacme Quinta in tax collection in Catalonia 

- Close-Reading: it's become clear that when Joan or Violant uses the word 'marvellam' or 'marvellada' to mean 'we are astonished,' this means that the recipient is about to be chastised; Violant uses the word 'flacament' to describe Berenguer d'Olms's actions thus far and that means 'weakly'; the word 'dessus', which in this sense means 'above all,' appearing in superscript might have been an intentional pun by the scribe

## What is this document doing?
- This document invokes shame as it scolds a knight for his lax service to the monarch.
- The document implies that the knight might choose to reject a job offer from the queen.

## Questions
- Was Berenguer d'Olms pushing back against Violant's exercise of power? Or was maybe that he was actually busy with other things?
- How sarcastic was Violant's intended tone in this letter?
- Had Violant heard about Berenger d'Olms behaving 'flacament' from Jacme Quinta, the treasury agent?
