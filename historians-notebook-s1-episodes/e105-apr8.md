## Today’s Document 
- Subject: criminal pursuit of a renegade nobleman in Valencia
- Date: April 8, 1387
- Day of the Week: Monday
- Language: Catalan
- Archival Reference Number: ACA CR R1751 f40r
- [Link to PARES](https://pares.mcu.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/show/4801766)
- Place: Barcelona
- Sender: Joan
- Recipients: Janerio Rabaca and fellow lawyers in Valencia

## Historical Thinking Notes
- Sourcing: Janerio Rabaca's title is micer, not mossen, and so in this document we see a large difference in status between the sender, the king, and the recipients; it appears that Berenguer de Vilaragut has been apprehended but the lawyers are dragging their feet on the prosecution; Joan, as king, uses the threat of his displeasure in an attempt to get the lawyers to stop delaying

- Contextualization: the modern scholar Paz Lloret researched the factional feuding in Valencia during this time period and it appears to have been going on long before the start of Joan's reign; in the Middle Ages, the ability of monarchs often struggled to put a stop to vendettas and clan violence, so the reluctance of the officials in Valencia might not be an expression of outright defiance against the new monarchs 

- Corroboration: in the document examined for [Episode 51](e51-feb13) Violant wrote to the Governor of Valencia, demanding results in the apprehension of Berenguer de Vilaragut and his supporters; Joan mentions other documents that have been sent to the lawyers and those records would go a long way corroborating Joan's claim of the severity of Berenguer de Vilaragut's crimes 

- Close-Reading: in lines 9-10, Joan tells the recipients that, after this letter, they can no longer claim ignorance; from that, we can pretty much infer that these lawyers have been delaying or dragging their feet when it comes to the prosecution of Berenguer Vilaragut

## What is this document doing?
- This document attempts to wipe away the veneer of ignorance claimed by the recipients.
- The document invokes royal authority in an effort to spur local authorities to act.

## Questions
- Why had the prosecution of Berenguer de Vilaragut been in Violant's portfolio in February and now in Joan's?
- What had made it so difficult for the authorities in Valencia to successfully prosecute Berenguer de Vilaragut?
- Would the recipients have been alarmed at such a scolding letter from the king or did they feel insulated from the royal authorities?
- Was the fine 2,000 florins, and if so, did that amount frighten the recipients of this letter?
- What injury to the 'fisc' had Berenguer de Vilaragut perpetrated?
