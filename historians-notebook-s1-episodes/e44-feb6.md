## Historical Thinking Notes
- Sourcing: it is likely that this town was part of Sibilla de Fortia's portfolio and that Violant is newly learning about the condition of the mill in this town; Violant's main concern would be to get the revenue flowing again and it appears to be an easy solution to provide a bit of financing

- Contextualization: the fourteenth-century documents of the royal family mention many infrastructure projects, ranging from military fortifications to canal re-routing; at times the documents order lower officials to spend the money for the project and other times the document indicates that funds will be sent; the larger context of the financial system of the Crown of Aragon determined much of the decision-making and financial crises in the 1380s put a strain on the availability of funds for projects like the mill in this document

- Corroboration: this document lacks any mention of obstacles to getting the money to Bernardo Torroni; other documents might reveal problems with funding streams

- Close-Reading: with only 'Yolans, etc.' starting the letter, the lack of formality and ornamentation might reflect the lower status of the recipient

## What is this document doing?
- This document maintains economic arrangements that connect the royal family in the capital city to a small business in a faraway town.
- The document asserts Violant's role as an economic manager of the territories in her portfolio.
