## Today’s Document 
- Subject: the legal and diplomatic fallout from a Catalan pirate's seizing of a Genoese ship
- Date: April 28, 1387
- Day of the Week: Sunday
- Language: Catalan
- Archival Reference Number: ACA CR R1751 f53r
- [Link to PARES](https://pares.mcu.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/show/4801766)
- Place: Barcelona
- Sender: Joan
- Recipient: the Catalan corsair Guillermo Raymundo, Count of Montcada and Agosto

## Historical Thinking Notes
- Sourcing: Guillermo Raymundo, Count of Montcada and Agosto, was at this time already a famous adventurer, having captured/rescued Maria of Sicily in 1380 so that she could marry Joan's nephew, Martí the Younger; the ongoing dispute over the act of piracy in 1386 does appear to count as highest level diplomacy, with the matter's inclusion in a 1390 peace treaty attesting to its impact; Joan might have felt a bit of satisfaction at the fact that at least on one occasion the act of piracy helps his realm at the expense of his rival; the selection of Luchino Scarampi to get involved as a mediator reflects Scarampi's past role as a peace treaty negotiator between the Crown of Aragon and Genoa, but also the level of trust that he has earned from Violant and Joan -- or possibly instead the very large amount of wealth that he has accumulated by this time

- Contextualization: the ongoing rivalry between the Crown of Aragon and Genoa at times escalated into military conflict but for most of the 1380s and 1390s it was made up of low-level proxy fighting and trade disputes

- Corroboration: the documents examined in [Episode 116](e116-apr19) and [Episode 135](e135-may8) corroborates the existence of an ongoing piracy problem for the Crown of Aragon, while this document demonstrates the ways that a pirate could further the interests of the Crown of Aragon; the document examined in [Episode 121](e121-apr24) provides another angle on the rivalry between the Crown of Aragon and Genoa

- Close-Reading: the toponym for Damascus conflicts with Zurita's idenfication of the seized cargo's source as Beirut; perhaps Damascus and Beirut were interchangeable for Joan at this time

## What is this document doing?
- This document only slightly gestures toward a condemnation of piracy.
- The document perpetuates the less than cordial relations between the Crown of Aragon and Genoa.
- The document entrusts Luchino Scarampi with another high level diplomatic task, further embedding him in the highest levels of state governance.
- The document demonstrates a capable monarch managing a diplomatic problem.

## Questions
- Did Joan approve of Guillermo Raymundo or consider him a nuisance?
- How much did Joan actually care about the restitution of the cargo to Genoa?
- Was the ship actually from Beirut?
- How did Luchino Scarampi deal with this dispute while also handling the betrothal negotiations in Avignon?
