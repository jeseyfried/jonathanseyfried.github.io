## Today’s Document 
- Subject: commission for an official royal messenger
- Date: April 7, 1387
- Day of the Week: Sunday (Easter Sunday)
- Language: Latin
- Archival Reference Number: ACA CR R1890 f161v
- [Link to PARES](https://pares.mcu.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/show/12758996)
- Place: Barcelona
- Sender: Joan
- Recipient: Johannes de Monte

## Historical Thinking Notes
- Sourcing: Joan, as king, held the power to appoint all sorts of officials and provide them with salaries; however, it is very possible that appointments like this were handled by his advisers, such as the chamberlain and that Joan simply signed off on them

- Contextualization: in the Late Middle Ages, the growth of the state, and the expansion of its bureaucracy, required more messengers and couriers

- Corroboration: the documents examined in [Episode 41](e41-feb3) and [Episode 42](e42-feb4) feature a messenger named Gerau who delivered personal messages for Joan and Violant and thus might have had much higher status than Johannes de Monte; there are also messengers directly mentioned in the documents examined in [Episode 47](e56-feb9) and [Episode 56](e56-fe18)

- Close-Reading: the use of the phrase 'latorem presentium' might indicate that Johannes de Monte has been present in the province of Aragon for a while and is part of this document's review of his well-established reputation

## What is this document doing?
- This document incorporates an individual person into the state apparatus.
- The document invests the messenger with the authority and legitimacy of the state.

## AI Usage
I used [Gemini](/historians-notebook-s1-episodes/pdfs/e104-gemini-transcription.pdf) for an initial transcription, which I then had [Claude](/historians-notebook-s1-episodes/pdfs/e104-claude-project-prompts.pdf) reconcile with its oWn initial transcription. Claude then produced [a translation](/historians-notebook-s1-episodes/pdfs/e104-claude-completed-translation.pdf) into English with footnotes. This episode features a great example of how errors can compound when using LLMs for automatic text recognition. In this case the 'ad' early in the document triggered Gemini to interpret the word as 'admiral' and then both LLMs got stuck with thinking that this document was about the navy in some way. In addition to the use of AI for this episode, I also used the online Latin dictionary, [Whitaker's Words](https://latin-words.com/).
