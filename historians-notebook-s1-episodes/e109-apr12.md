## Today’s Document 
- Subject: the recall of the knight Galceran de Vilanova from Castile
- Date: April 12, 1387
- Day of the Week: Friday
- Language: Castilian
- Archival Reference Number: ACA CR R1751 f43r
- [Link to PARES](https://pares.mcu.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/show/4801766)
- Place: Barcelona
- Sender: Joan
- Recipient: Juan I of Castile

## Historical Thinking Notes
- Sourcing: as a fellow king, Joan might have sympathized with Juan I's concerns over the impending invasion by John of Gaunt; however, this did not stop Joan from prioritizing his own military needs with the rebellion brewing in Sardinia

- Contextualization: maintaing a hold on Sardinia remained a constaint challenge for the monarchs of the Crown of Aragon in the fourteenth century but its value as a breadbasket made it impossible to let go of; see the Contextualization notes in [Episode 90](e90-mar24) for more precise info

- Corroboration: in light of [yesterday's episode](e108-apr11), Joan was almost certainly aware of the King of Castile's concerns about an incoming invasion; the document examined in [Episode 90](e90-mar24) corroborates Joan's concern about rebellion in Sardinia, as he sought funds from the Bishop of Zaragoza to pay for troops to send there; more information would be needed from Castile to understand the impact of the decision to recall Galceran de Vilanova

- Close-Reading: Joan sends this letter out in Castilian and refers to Juan I as 'muy caro hermano' and perhaps this letter contains more than the boilerplate pleasantries; the conspicuous absence of any mention of the impending invasion by John of Guant might indicate a lack of coordination between Violant's advisers and Joan's advisers

## What is this document doing?
- This document invokes comraderie to soften the blow of the action taken. 
- The document reveals that the sender felt the need to supply a legitimate reason for the recall notice.

## Questions
- Why didn't Joan say anything about Juan I's troubling situation of an invasion of the English coming in from Portugal?
- How would the recall of Galceran de Vilanova have been seen by Juan I? A devastating blow? Possibly a relief?
- Did Galceran de Vilanova have a large entourage?
- What were Joan's expectations for how Galceran de Vilanova would contribute to the fight for control over Sardinia?
- Did Violant know about this document?
- What does this document demonstrate about the level of power or status difference between Castile and the Crown of Aragon at this time? Does it show that Castile was weak?
