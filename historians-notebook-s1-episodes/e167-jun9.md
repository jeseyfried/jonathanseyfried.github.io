## Today’s Document 
- Subject: the need to add up the cost of damages incurred by Kingdom of Navarre
- Date: June 9, 1387
- Day of the Week: Sunday
- Language: Latin
- Archival Reference Number: ACA CR R1954 f3v
- [Link to PARES](https://pares.mcu.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/show/12772131)
- Place: Barcelona
- Sender: Joan
- Recipients: Sancho Martinez de Biota, Governor of Araton, and Michael Capelli a magistrate of Zaragoza

## Historical Thinking Notes
- Sourcing: the probata for this document indicates that it was composed by the queen on behalf of the king, even though it is in Joan's register; the recipient was earlier in the year upbraided by Joan for not moving fast enough to confiscate property of Bernat da Fortià; the immediate cause of this document is information received from the King of Navarre about 'dampnatge,' damage, that has been brought about in his land

- Contextualization: although I was not able to figure out from this document what caused the damage in Navarre, it could very well be mercenaries passing through or factional violence; the hinterland near the Pyrenees, especially around Jaca, was known for factional violence in the fourteenth century[^mifp]

[^mifp]:María Isabel Falcon Pérez, “La Salvaguarda de La Paz En Las Montañas de Jaca,” *Aragón En La Edad Media*, no. 20 (2008): 287–99.

- Corroboration: today's document makes for an excellent comparison to the one examined in [Episode 76](e76-mar10) since that earlier letter to the same recipient is filled with invective and castigation; [Episode 155](e155-may28) discussed violence in the northeast part of Aragon, possibly near the border of Navarre; the damage could also have been caused by the mercenaries that Violant paid off to not journey through the Crown of Aragon on the way to Castile, as discussed in [Episode 140](e140-may13)

- Close-Reading: the frequent use of the word 'marché' indicates that the frontier zone between the Crown of Aragon and Navarre saw significant damage; this implies a far less monitored and defended border between the two lands than might otherwise be assumed from looking at historical maps; this document offers a wide range of vocabulary for injury, destruction, and damage

## What is this document doing?
- This document puts state resources into investigating and tabulating destruction.
- The document acknowledges the Crown of Aragon as the entity responsible for restitution.

## Additional Notes
Gerenti is the present active participle, in the dative or ablative case, of the verb gero, gerere. As an [online Latin textbook explains](https://lingualatina.github.io/textbook/2021-2022/10-participles/present-active/), the present active participle is when a verb is used to describe what a noun is doing, such as the word 'walking' in the sentence, 'the walking man suddenly jumped.' My explanation of this in today's podcast was a muddled but generall accurate.
