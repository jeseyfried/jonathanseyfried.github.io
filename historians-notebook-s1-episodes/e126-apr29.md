- Subject: initiating an intercession of holy prayer for Joan's health and an urgent summons for a doctor
- Date: April 29, 1387
- Day of the Week: Monday
- Language: Catalan
- Archival Reference Number for Violant's letter: ACA CR R2053 f7r-v
- [Link to PARES for Violant's Letter](https://pares.mcu.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/show/12778718)

- Sender: Violant (Joan's register has Violant's scribe in the probata)

## Historical Thinking Notes for Violant's Documents
- Sourcing: the recipient, as a Cardinal, would have the authority to instruct churches to say prayers for the king; Violant, as the wife of the king, probably faced intense emotional pressure at this moment as well as the prospect of political danger

- Contextualization: according to widely held Christian beliefs at this time, the prayers of nuns and monks in religious orders throughout the land would have an intercessionary effect

- Corroboration: Violant directly contradicts her letter to James from four days ago, [Episode 122](e122-apr25), when today she writes, 'qui segons sapiat se longament ha es malalt,' who you know has been sick for a long time

- Close-Reading: Violant uses the phrase 'cort celestial' to signal the field on which intercessionary powers might play out to produce a favorable outcome in the recovery of her husband's health

## Historical Thinking Notes for Joan's Document
- Sourcing: as one of the most powerful people in the land, the king would theoretically have access to the best physicians; Joan seeks out the Muslim doctor in Xàtiva for that reason and religious boundaries do not matter in the context of a severe illness

- Contextualization: medical knowledge did cross the boundaries of religious communities, as has been demonstrated convincincly by the research of Carmen Caballero-Navas[^ccn]

[^ccn]:Carmen Caballero-Navas, “The Care of Women’s Health and Beauty: An Experience Shared by Medieval Jewish and Christian Women,” *Journal of Medieval History* 34, no. 2 (2008): 146–63.

- Corroboration: there is evidence that the royal family regularly employed Jewish physicians but it is far less common to see a record of a Muslim doctor 

- Close-Reading: the admonition against delay underscores the severity of Joan's health crisis; this is further confirmed by the instruction to have the doctor travel 'night and day'

## What are these documents doing?
- These documents communicate a medical emergency, calling out for both spiritual and material help. 
- The documents reflect a willingness to employ diverse methods in response to disease.

## Questions
- What had Joan, or his court, heard about Abrahim, the Muslim doctor in Xàtiva?
- Was Abrahim found? Did he make the journey to Barcelona?
- What does it mean that Violant's scribe is writing in Joan's register?
- How did the kinship relation between Joan and James factor into Violant's letter to him? Why did Violant not write to other ecclesiastical leaders in the realm?
- How did Violant choose the convents and monasteries for her next letter? Did a list of those already exist?


## Additional Notes
According to Jerónimo Zurita, April 29th is also the date of the execution of two of Sibilla da Fortià's advisors. However, I cannot find an archival source for this (neither through my own research nor through citations in secondary sources).
