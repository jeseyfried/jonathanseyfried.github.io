## Today’s Document 
- Subject: Violant thanks several monasteries for their prayers
- Date: February 5, 1387
- Day of the Week: Tuesday
- Language: Catalan
- Archival Reference Number: ACA CR R1819 f79v
- [Link to PARES](https://pares.mcu.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/show/4801968)
- Place: Barcelona
- Sender: Violant
- Recipients: several monasteries in the Crown of Aragon

## Historical Thinking Notes
- Sourcing: as queen, Violant can send substantial resources to this monastery, ensuring its continuity; the purpose is not only to express gratitude and to foster a network connection, but is also transactional in the sense that the monks' prayers are most likely interpreted by Violant as having real-world effects for Joan's health (see Contextualization directly below)

- Contextualization: this document reflects the larger context of how people in the Middle Ages in Western Europe believed in a direct connection between the divine and the events that happened in their lives; when Violant thanks the Carthusian monks for their prayers, she invokes a knowledge of the world and human existence in which the intercession between humans and the divine is a feature of reality; this differs from how we consider the influence of prayer in curing illness in modern society, where the connection is seen as a feature of an individual's belief system and not a shared reality; saints' relics commonly provided an intercessory role along with prayers

- Corroboration: several other charters and documents examined in this podcast have demonstrated the connection between the monarchs and religious institutions; here, though, is a personal touch that is largely absent from the documents that recommended people to fill vacancies

- Close-Reading: Violant uses the word 'malalties' to describe what has happened to Joan at the outset of their reign; in other letters Joan's illness was referred to as misfortune or difficulties

## What is this document doing?
- This document reinforces, and operates within, a discourse of intercession between the divine and the mundane world.
- The document also asserts Violant's active response to Joan's illness, managing the spiritual aspects of his recovery.

## Questions
- What kind of information did Violant receive about the prayers that the monks made for Joan? Their frequency? Their content? 
- Did Violant see the monks' prayers as directly counteracting a curse or act of evil sorcery that had been directed at Joan?
- How would the monasteries react to the gratitude expressed in this letter? 
- Was this letter pro forma, something they were used to getting from monarchs and other elites or was it unusual to get a letter like this?
- Did Violant use 'malalties' in any correspondence before February 5th to refer to Joan's impairment?
- Are there any monasteries absent from this list that would normally have been included?
- Did Joan ever write to the monasteries thanking them for their prayers? Would there have been a gendered aspect to that?

## Additional Notes
In this episode I mention a letter from April 29, 1387, transcribed in an article by Dawn Bratsch-Prince in which Violant again thanks a long list of monasteries for praying for Joan.[^dbp]

[^dbp]:Dawn E. Prince, “A Reappraisal of the Correspondence of Violant de Bar (1365-1431),” *Catalan Review: International Journal of Catalan Culture* 8, nos. 1–2 (1994): 295–312, at 309-310.

The monasteries listed as recipients of this letter include [Scala Dei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoixa_d%27Escaladei), a Carthusian monastery in the southwest of Catalonia, and [Porta Coeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Coeli_Charterhouse), a Carthusian monastery near Valencia.

For example, ChatGPT called this letter 'very Violant' and makes a (malformed) argument for its use in a historical narrative.
