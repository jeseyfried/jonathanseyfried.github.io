## Today’s Document 
- Subject: request for a reprieve in feudal obligations for the knight Hugo [San Pau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Pau)
- Date: June 13, 1387
- Day of the Week: Thursday
- Language: Latin
- Archival Reference Number: ACA CR R1867 f2r-v
- [Link to PARES](https://pares.mcu.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/show/12758995)
- Place: Barcelona
- Sender: Joan
- Recipient: James I, King of Cyprus

## Historical Thinking Notes
- Sourcing: James I's sister-in-law was [Eleanor of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_of_Aragon,_Queen_of_Cyprus), the first cousin of Pere the Ceremonious, Joan's father; by 1387, James has turned against Genoa and this probably explains Joan's tender address to him as 'consanguinous carissime,' dearest kinsman; throughout 1387 and the subsequent early years of his reign Joan kept trying to raise money for his coronation and also to become healthy enough for it

- Contextualization: Genoa had long dominated Cyprus and interfered in its internal politics; with Genoa as the chief rival of the Crown of Aragon for dominance in Mediterranean trade, Pere the Ceremonious took every chance he got during his reign to marry off a royal woman to a power player in a Mediterreanean island; there were longstanding traditions by which knights declared fealty to a particular lord or king and then carried out obligations, although 'feudalism' is a term that has become very difficult to define across differing contexts of Western Europe and the Mediterranean region

- Corroboration: thus far, the only other time Joan admitted to a disability was the document examined in [Episode 58](e58-feb20); for other episodes that touch on the way Joan's disability was hidden or revealed, see the episodes [tagged for illness](https://jonathanseyfried.net/historians-notebook/tags/?tag=illness)

- Close-Reading: Joan chose to use the phrase 'nostri corporis distenciam' to describe the severe illness that forced him to delay his coronation and this word choice lacks attestation in [Logeion](https://logeion.uchicago.edu/distentus) other than possibly related to distentus, meaning occupied or swelled out 

## What is this document doing?
- This document connects the physical state of the body of the king to an act of diplomacy.
- The document acknowledges the traditions of feudal obligations across the Mediterranean region.

## Questions
- Was Hugo de San Pau eager to return to Cyprus or more interested in finding reasons to extend his stay in Barcelona?
- Did Joan find it uncomfortable to put the fact of his disability into writing?
- Had there been a realistic chance that the coronation could have happened in the first half of 1387, given the financial circumstances?
- What were the current expectations for the scheduling of the coronation ceremony?
