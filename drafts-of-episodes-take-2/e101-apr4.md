## Today’s Document 
- Subject: marriage negotiations for Yolande of Aragon
- Date: April 4, 1387
- Day of the Week: Thursday
- Language: Latin and Catalan
- Archival Reference Number: ACA CR R2056 f96r-v
- [Link to PARES](https://pares.mcu.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/show/12777819)
- Place: Barcelona
- Sender: Violant
- Recipients: Pope and Luchino Scarampi

## Historical Thinking Notes
- Sourcing: Violant's upbringing in the royal court of Charles V of France prepared her to communicate confidently with high status figure such as the pope and the titular Queen of Naples; these two documents show that Violant considers herself in a position to direct the deal-making and to have Luchino Scarampi carry out the necessary maneuvers

- Contextualization: medieval elite marriages were highly complex negotiations, which could be conducted at diplomatic nerve centers such as Avignon, and the consequence of a marriage deal affected not just the immediate fates of the two people being married but also the future stability of the realms involved; the opportunity to have Yolande of Aragon marry Louis II of Anjou had the potential to bring Naples into the orbit of Crown of Aragon and combined with Sicily would have raised the Crown's profile as a Mediterranean power player

- Corroboration: the documents examined in [Episode 66](e66-feb28), [Episode 70](e70-mar4), and [Episode 72](e72-mar6) established Luchino Scarampi's credentials as lead negotiator for the marriage and also spelled out some of the details of the deal

- Close-Reading: the second letter purports a willingness for Violant to conform to the wishes of the Queen of Naples, but Violant's willingness to directly state that the Queen of Naples does not care, 'no cura,' about the Pope might indicate a certain impatience and lack of deference

## What is this document doing?
- These documents reinforce Luchino Scarampi's position as a representative of the king and queen of the Crown of Aragon.
- The second document positions Violant as accommodating to the Queen of Naples while also retaining her own status as a director of the negotiations. 

## Questions
- Was Violant frustrated with the Queen of Naples?
- Had this marriage negotiation run into trouble or was this a sign of progress?
- Why did Luchino Scarampi need another letter to the Pope to confirm that he was representing the wishes of Violant and Joan? 
- Was the Pope getting sidelined in this deal-making process?
- What parts of the marriage negotiation were the most troublesome?
