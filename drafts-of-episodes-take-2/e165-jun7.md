## Today’s Document 
- Subject: the new tithes authorized by the Pope for church institutions to pay the Crown
- Date: June 7, 1387
- Day of the Week: Friday
- Language: Catalan
- Archival Reference Number: ACA CR R1972 f121r
- [Link to PARES](https://pares.mcu.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/show/12779046)
- Place: Barcelona
- Sender: Joan
- Recipients: Bishop of Lleida

## Historical Thinking Notes
- Sourcing: the creator of this document, as a manifestation of the corporate monarchy, brings the full weight of the state apparatus toward getting compliance in the collection of this money from the bishop; the recipient, perhaps of lower status compared to other ecclesiastical leaders in the realm, likely did not feel he could resist this new financial imposition

- Contextualization: in the fourteenth century, tithes knowns as 'decimas' were paid to the state by various ecclesiastical institutions within the realm[^mvl]

[^mvl]:Marta VanLandingham, *Transforming the State: King, Court and Political Culture in the Realms of Aragon (1213-1387)*, (Brill, 2002), 122.

- Corroboration: the thread of Johan de Muntros and the Pope's authorization of this new tithe payment from ecclesiastical institutions to the royal treasury has been discussed in the document examined in [Episode 90](e90-mar24), [Episode 137](e137-may10), [Episode 138](e138-may11), [Episode 139](e139-may12), and [Episode 149](e149-may22); a corroboration of these documents would involve tracing the development of this new form of revenue for the Crown and noting any problems or obstacles at different stages of its implementation

- Close-Reading: nothing in the word choices of this document suggests that the king is accusing the bishop of delinquency or an intentional refusal to pay; the document reads as a simple record-keeping measure

## What is this document doing?
- This document positions the bishop as a debtor to the king.
- The document increases the power of Johan de Muntros over the revenue streams heading to the royal treasury.

## Questions
- Does the fact of this partial payment indicate that the Bishop of Lleida was having trouble coming up with the money for the new tithe?
- How did this new tithe strain the relationship between ecclesiastical institutions and the state?
- Were there any subtleties in this document that would have caused offense to the bishop?
- Did Johan de Muntros come across to the Bishop of Lleida as a responsible and respectable representative of the king?
